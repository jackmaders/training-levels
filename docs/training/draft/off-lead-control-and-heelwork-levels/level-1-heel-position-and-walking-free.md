# Off-Lead Control Level 1: Heel Position & Walking Free

## Comebefores

Complete [Level 2 Focus](../../markdown/level-2/focus.md), [Level 2 Target](../../markdown/level-2/target.md), and [Level 3 Come](../../markdown/level-3/come.md) in the Main Quest. Your dog should already know how to follow a hand target and stay engaged with you around mild distractions.

## Where We're Going

- Step 1: Dog finds the exact "pocket" beside your left leg via pivot or target (`Heel`).
- Step 2: Dog returns cleanly to your side from front or distance (KC Silver & Gold _Return to Side_).
- Step 3: Dog maintains heel position for 1–5 steps forward plus crisp halts.
- Step 4: Dog tracks your left leg through left turns, right turns, about-turns, and pace changes.
- Step 5: Dog completes 1–2 minutes of **Walk Free Beside Handler** off-lead (KC Gold Exercise 3).

## Think About Precision Heelwork

Unlike **Lazy Leash** (where your dog walks comfortably anywhere within the slack of the lead), **Heel** means your dog's shoulder aligns neatly beside your left hip/leg—whether moving slow, fast, turning, or halting.

Because heelwork asks for real concentration, **keep reps very short (10–30 seconds)** and pay generously right at your left trouser seam so your dog builds a magnetic draw to that exact spot.

## About the Cues

- **Heel (or Close):** Means _"Lock onto my left leg and match my pace/turns until I release you."_
- **Free (or OK):** Releases your dog from heelwork back into normal movement.

## Steps

### Step 1: Finding the "pocket" (pivot to heel position — `Heel`).

Before walking anywhere, teach your dog exactly _where_ heel position is while standing still:

1. **Hand-Lure or Target Method:** Stand straight with a treat in your left hand (or use an inverted rubber bowl/perch under your dog's front paws so she pivots her hind end freely). Guide her nose slightly past your left hip, then sweep your left hand forward to bring her parallel beside your left leg, facing the same direction as you.
2. **Click** the instant her body lines up straight beside your left shin (sitting or standing squarely), and feed right against your left trouser seam.
3. Repeat until she swings smoothly into the left-side pocket with just a tiny left-hand flick. Add the cue **Heel** as she finishes swinging into place.

---

### Step 2: Return to handler's side from front or distance.

Both KC Silver (_Rejoin Handler_) and KC Gold (_Return to Handler's Side_) test calling your dog neatly back into heel position:

1. Stand 3 feet in front of your dog (sitting on a wait), say **Heel**, and sweep your left hand back just past your left hip to invite her around behind or directly into your left side. **Click** as she lands straight beside your leg, and feed at your left hip.
2. Gradually increase starting distance to 6 feet, then 10–15 feet away.
3. Practice calling her into **Heel** from different angles (in front of you, slightly to your right, slightly behind you) so she learns to find the left-side pocket from anywhere.

#### Try It Cold

When your dog is wandering 10 feet away in a quiet yard or room, stand tall, say **Heel** once, and see if she trots straight into your left pocket without extra prompting.

---

### Step 3: One-step to five-step close heelwork.

Now add forward motion one tiny slice at a time:

1. Start in heel position. Say **Heel**, take **1 clear step forward** with your left foot, and halt cleanly.
2. The instant she steps forward with you and stops beside your leg (offering a sit or clean stand), **Click** and feed right at your left leg seam.
3. Build to 2 steps, 3 steps, and 5 steps—always rewarding at your trouser seam rather than reaching out in front of you (reaching forward pulls the dog out of position!).
4. Release frequently with **OK!** and a tossed treat so heelwork feels lively and fun.

---

### Step 4: Turns and pace changes.

Keep your dog glued to your left knee through changes in direction and speed:

- **About-Turns (180°):** Pivoting right (away from the dog) or doing a tight U-turn left (using a hand target at first so she tucks her hips neatly). Click right as she finishes the turn beside you!
- **90° Left & Right Turns:** Start with single turns after 3 straight steps—click the moment she pivots her shoulders to stay aligned with your leg.
- **Pace Changes:** Shift smoothly from normal walk $\rightarrow$ 5 slow steps $\rightarrow$ 5 brisk steps $\rightarrow$ crisp halt, clicking each clean transition.

---

### Step 5: Walk free beside handler (off-lead heelwork — KC Gold).

In a securely fenced area or indoor training room, practice **walking free**:

1. Do 2 short warm-up patterns on a completely slack lead (leaving the clip loose).
2. Unclip the lead, drape it over your shoulder, cue **Heel**, and walk a 30–60 second pattern including straight lines, one left turn, one right turn, an about-turn, a pace change, and two halts.
3. Keep rewards upbeat and right at your left hip. Gradually extend duration to 2 minutes and add mild distractions (walking past a cone, a quiet person, or a settled dog).

#### Comeafters

Always finish an off-lead heel pattern with a cheerful **OK!** release and a quick game of **Get It** toy play or sniff break so your dog loves working off-lead with you.
