# Off-Lead Control Level 2: Emergency Stop (_Stop the Dog_)

## Comebefores

Complete [Level 3 Sit](../../markdown/level-3/sit.md), [Level 3 Down](../../markdown/level-3/down.md), and [Level 4 Come](../../markdown/level-4/come.md) in the Main Quest. Your dog must already know a crisp **Down** (or **Sit**) from a stationary distance before interrupting active running.

## Where We're Going

- Step 1: Dog performs a snappy back-step stop 1–2 steps away (`Drop` / `Stop`).
- Step 2: Dog drops mid-recall at 10 feet and holds position until you walk up to pay.
- Step 3: Team uses the **80/20 Variable Reward Rule** so recalls stay fast.
- Step 4: Dog stops cleanly while running _away_ from you toward a mat or toy.
- Step 5: Dog executes a full **Gold-Standard Emergency Stop** at 15–20+ feet outdoors.

## Think About the Emergency Stop

Tested in the Kennel Club Gold Award (_Stop the Dog_), an emergency stop asks your dog to brake instantly mid-stride and freeze in place—whether she is trotting toward you, chasing a rolling leaf, or heading toward a road.

Two mechanical rules make or break this behavior:

1. **Always Pay _In Place_:** Never call your dog _forward_ to you to reward an emergency stop! If you click and make her run to you for the treat, she learns to creep forward through the stop. **Walk out to her and feed her right where she froze.**
2. **Protect Your Recall Speed (The 80/20 Rule):** If you stop your dog on every single recall, she will start trotting cautiously halfway in anticipation. Keep 4 out of 5 recalls full-speed all the way to you, and mix emergency stops in unpredictably.

## About the Cues

Pick one crisp, unmistakable cue—either **Drop** (for an instant down), **Wait**, or **Stop** (if you accept a fast sit, down, or stand freeze, though a flat **Down** is safest in emergencies)—paired with a big, high **raised flat palm** visual signal that your dog can see from 20+ feet away.

## Steps

### Step 1: Snappy back-step stop (1–2 steps away).

Build speed and reflex first right in front of you:

1. Stand facing your dog 2 feet away, holding a treat visibly or having her happily engaged.
2. Walk backward 2 brisk steps inviting her to trot forward with you—then suddenly freeze your feet, shoot one flat palm straight up, and say **Drop** cleanly once.
3. The instant her elbows or hips hit the ground, **Click**, step _forward_ to where she dropped, and feed 2–3 high-value treats right between her front paws (keeping her anchored in place).
4. Release with **OK!** and repeat 4–5 times until her drop looks like a spring-loaded reflex.

---

### Step 2: Interrupting a short recall (10 feet).

Now ask for the stop while she has real forward momentum:

1. Leave your dog in a **Wait** 10 feet away.
2. Walk back, face her, and call **Come!** cheerfully.
3. After she takes 2–3 eager strides toward you, plant your feet, flash your flat-hand signal up, and say **Drop** crisply.
4. **Click** the instant she brakes into a down (or freezes cleanly), walk all the way out to her spot, and jackpot her **right where she stopped**!

> [!NOTE]
> **Problem: She keeps sliding or walking two extra steps toward me**
> First, make sure your body isn't leaning forward—lean slightly _tall_ as you give the stop signal. Second, if she creeps forward after stopping, do not pay at your feet; calmly ask for another drop where she is, wait 1 second, and reward only once she commits to braking sooner on the next rep.

---

### Step 3: Variable reward (80% full recall, 20% stop).

Protect your recall enthusiasm using unpredictability:

- Run 10 recalls at 10–15 feet across a session.
- On **8 reps**, let her run all the way in at full speed for a huge party at your side!
- On **2 random reps**, throw in **Drop** mid-run, walk out to jackpot her in place, and then release her with **Come!** to finish the recall for a second bonus reward!

---

### Step 4: Stopping while moving _away_ from the handler.

In real life (and Gold tests), dogs are often moving _away_ from you when you need to stop them:

1. Set a target mat (or a motionless "dead" toy) 10–15 feet ahead.
2. Send your dog toward the mat/toy (**Go To Mat** or **Get It**).
3. Halfway out (at 5–8 feet), say **Drop** sharply and raise your hand.
4. **Click** the moment she hits the brakes and drops! Walk up beside her, feed 1 treat in place, and then say **OK — Go To Mat!** (or **Get It!**) so finishing the trip to the mat/toy becomes the massive life reward for obeying the stop!

---

### Step 5: Full Gold-Standard Emergency Stop (15–20+ feet).

Take the skill outdoors on a secure field or long line:

1. Practice interrupting both recalls and send-aways at 15–20 feet (using a slightly louder, carrying voice cue plus the tall arm signal).
2. Add mild real-world distractions—tossing a dead ball off to the side, having a friend stand nearby, or working on grass.
3. Require zero forward creeping for 3–5 seconds while you walk out to reward her in place.

#### Try It Cold

During an ordinary yard play session or off-lead walk in a fenced field, casually say **Drop** + raise your hand while she is trotting 15 feet ahead of you—then celebrate wildly right at her spot!
