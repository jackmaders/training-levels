# Nosework Level 1: Foundation & Introducing Odor

## Comebefores

Before starting Nosework Level 1, your dog should understand your marker (clicker or verbal **"Yes!"** means a treat is coming) and be comfortable taking treats calmly from your hand. If you are working through the main [Training Levels](../../markdown/index.md), [Level 1 Zen](../../markdown/level-1/zen.md) is helpful so your dog does not mug your treat pouch, though you should never punish enthusiasm during nosework.

## Where We're Going

- Step 1: Dog sources a paired hide (odor + food) at nose level.
- Step 2: Dog back-chains a short, independent approach (1–3 feet) to a paired box.
- Step 3: Handler practices _"You Search, I Sit"_ to eliminate body-language crutches.
- Step 4: Dog shifts from paired odor to unpaired odor, paid directly at source.
- Step 5: Dog locates a single hide placed in varied spots around a room.

## Equipment

- **Treats:** Very small, soft, high-value treats that can be swallowed quickly without frantic chewing.
- **Target Odor Container:** A small perforated tin or breathable tea ball holding a cotton swab scented with target odor (or a distinct dried herb/tea bag for purely recreational indoor games). Keep a pair of tweezers or dedicated gloves for handling the swab so your hands do not smear odor everywhere.
- **Starter Box:** One shallow open cardboard box or sturdy low tray. Shallow boxes make it easy for your dog to see and access the tin comfortably without frustration.
- **Non-Slip Surface:** A rug, runner, or carpeted room so your dog has confident footing.

## Think About Nosework Foundation

Many traditional programs spend weeks having dogs search empty cardboard boxes for hidden food before ever introducing a target odor. While food searches are great enrichment, dogs who only hunt food in boxes sometimes develop "box-smashing" habits—guessing, pawing, or knocking boxes over without truly sniffing.

In the **Scentsabilities** approach, we introduce target odor right away using **paired hides** (target odor plus food right next to or inside the same container). Through classical conditioning, the dog quickly learns that _the smell of the target odor itself predicts an amazing payout_. From there, we use **reward delivery at source**—bringing our treat hand directly to the odor tin—so the dog learns that the exact location of the smell is where the treasure appears.

Keep every session **short (3–5 minutes)** and end while your dog is still eager. Sniffing uses tremendous mental energy, and short, upbeat sessions keep drive and focus high.

## About the Cues

There are two cues in Nosework Level 1:

1. **The Search Ritual (Context Cue):** Getting out the box and odor tin, or asking your dog to wait on his mat while you set up, naturally signals that the sniffing game is about to start.
2. **The Verbal Cue — `Search` (or `Find It`):** Say your cue calmly once right as you present the box or release your dog to hunt. Do not chant _"Find it, find it, look here!"_—repeating cues turns you into a crutch and pulls your dog's focus off his nose and onto you.

## Steps

### Step 1: The dog sources a paired hide at nose level.

In a quiet room with few distractions, place your target odor tin inside a shallow open box, and drop 3–4 tiny high-value treats right next to (or directly on/around) the tin.

Present the open box near your dog's nose (2–6 inches away) and calmly say **Search**. As his nose dips into the box to sniff the odor and eat the treats, **Click** (or mark **"Yes!"**) and deliver 1–2 extra treats right at the tin before picking up the box for a 5-second reset.

Repeat 4–5 times. Keep your hands still and quiet—let your dog figure out that putting his nose right at the tin makes great things happen.

#### Try It Cold

Wait an hour, bring out the paired box in a calm room, set it just in front of your dog, and say **Search**. He should immediately orient his nose toward the tin without needing you to point.

> [!NOTE]
> **Problem: My dog paws at the box or tries to grab the tin**
> If your dog uses his paws or mouth on the box, calmly hold the rim of the box steady with one hand or switch to a heavier ceramic bowl/tray with the tin taped safely inside. Click the _microsecond_ his nose touches near the tin _before_ a paw lifts, and deliver treats low at the tin.

---

### Step 2: The dog back-chains a short, independent approach (1–3 feet).

Once your dog dives happily into the box when it is right under his nose, introduce **back-chaining**—starting from the endpoint (nose on odor) and adding tiny increments of distance backward.

1. Place the paired box (odor tin + 2–3 treats) 1–2 feet in front of your dog.
2. Say **Search**. Wait quietly while he moves forward on his own to put his nose on the tin.
3. **Click** as his nose reaches the tin and feed an extra bonus treat right at the tin.
4. Reset the dog by gently tossing a low-value reset treat a few feet away, move the box slightly (2–3 feet away), and repeat.

#### Comeafters

Gradually vary whether the box is straight ahead, to the left, or to the right so your dog practices turning toward the box independently.

---

### Step 3: "You Search, I Sit" — eliminating handler crutches.

Dogs are masters at reading human eyes, leaning shoulders, and pointing fingers. If you lean toward the box or stare at it, your dog will look at _you_ instead of using his nose.

1. Sit in a chair in the room with your hands resting calmly in your lap.
2. Place the paired box 2–4 feet away within easy view.
3. Say **Search** once, keep your eyes softly fixed on a neutral spot (not staring directly at the tin), and **do not move, point, or speak**.
4. Wait out any polite staring at you. The instant your dog turns his head away from you and puts his nose into the box, **Click** and lean in smoothly to feed right at the tin.

> [!TIP]
> **Training Tip: Stop Helping!**
> If your dog looks back at you asking _"Are you sure?"_, resist the urge to point or repeat the cue. Wait silently for 5–10 seconds. Learning that you will not give away the answer is the moment true independent hunting begins.

---

### Step 4: The dog shifts from paired to unpaired odor (paid at source).

Now that the target odor strongly predicts food, remove the food from inside the box so only the target odor tin remains.

1. Place the **empty** (odor-only) tin inside the shallow box. Make sure no loose crumbs are left inside.
2. Present the box and say **Search**.
3. The exact split-second your dog's nose touches or lingers over the odor tin, **Click!**
4. Immediately bring your treat hand _all the way down to the tin_ and feed your dog 2–3 treats one at a time **right at the tin** (so his nose stays glued to the odor source while eating).

If your dog seems confused because there is no free food inside the box, slide one step backward (_Chutes and Ladders_): put a single treat back alongside the tin for 2 reps, then try odor-only again.

#### Try It Cold

Set down an unpaired (odor-only) box at the start of a fresh session without doing any paired warm-ups first. Give the **Search** cue. If he puts his nose straight on the tin and waits for his click and at-source payout, he truly recognizes the target odor.

---

### Step 5: The dog locates a single hide in varied spots around a room.

Change the visual picture so your dog learns to follow his nose rather than memorizing one box spot:

- Tuck the tin just inside the fold of a clean towel on the floor.
- Place the box partly behind a heavy chair leg or beside a cushion so the tin is out of direct line-of-sight.
- Swap the shallow box for a small paper bag lying flat on its side, or place the tin under an overturned colander/vented plastic cup.

Move placements gradually around a quiet room, click the moment the nose reaches the closest accessible point to the tin, and feed right at source.
