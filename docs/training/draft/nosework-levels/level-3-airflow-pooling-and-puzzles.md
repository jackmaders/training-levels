# Nosework Level 3: Airflow, Pooling & Odor Puzzles

## Comebefores

Complete [Nosework Level 1](./level-1-foundation-and-odor.md) and [Nosework Level 2](./level-2-discrimination-and-commitment.md). Your dog should reliably discriminate a target odor tin from blank decoys and hold his nose at source for 1–2 seconds for at-source rewards.

## Where We're Going

- Step 1: Dog works a moving odor plume (upwind vs. downwind approach).
- Step 2: Dog works out of an **odor pool** to locate true source.
- Step 3: Dog commits to an **inaccessible hide** without pawing or frustration.
- Step 4: Dog checks **elevated hides** above nose height.
- Step 5: Dog resolves **converging odor** from two nearby hides.

## Think About Scent Theory & Odor Puzzles

Drawing on Stacy Barnett's background in chemical engineering and scent dynamics, **odor does not move in straight lines**. Air currents from doorways, heating/AC vents, warm sunlight, and solid furniture push scent molecules into swirling plumes and pockets.

Understanding three key odor phenomena prevents you from misreading your dog:

1. **Odor Plumes & Change of Behavior (COB):** Dogs rarely smell a tin directly from across a room; they hit a drifting scent plume first. Watch for subtle Changes of Behavior—a sharp head snap, closed-mouth deep sniffing, ear twitch, or slowing down—that tell you _"He is in odor!"_ even before he reaches source.
2. **Odor Pooling:** When airflow hits a wall, baseboard, corner, or the underside of a chair, odor drops and collects in a **pool** away from the actual tin. Inexperienced handlers often mistake lingering at an odor pool for a final alert and accidentally click or praise too early.
3. **Inaccessible & Converging Hides:** Sometimes a dog cannot physically touch the tin (it is sealed inside a vented box or tucked behind a grate), or two hides sit close enough that their scent cones overlap (**convergence**). Patiently letting your dog work through these puzzles builds tremendous confidence and mental stamina.

## About the Cues

Continue using **Search** once at the start of the puzzle. Your most important "cue" in Level 3 is **doing nothing** when your dog hits pooled odor—standing or sitting quietly gives him space to work his way to true source.

## Steps

### Step 1: Observing airflow (downwind vs. upwind approach).

Set a single unpaired odor tin near a gentle indoor air current—such as near a cracked door, an AC/heat vent, or 4–5 feet in front of a small quiet fan on its lowest setting (never blowing hard enough to startle your dog).

1. Start your dog **downwind** of the tin so the breeze carries the scent plume toward him.
2. Say **Search** and watch closely without intervening.
3. Notice the exact moment his nose catches the plume (head lift, focused sniffing) and how he traces the narrowing scent cone back upstream to the tin.
4. **Click** and feed at the tin only once he reaches actual source—not when he first catches the plume in mid-air.

#### Try It Cold

Run the same setup from the **upwind** side (behind the tin relative to the gentle airflow). Notice how your dog may initially pass the tin before catching the plume downwind and circling back to source.

---

### Step 2: Working out of an odor pool.

Create a deliberate odor pool so _you_ can practice staying quiet while your dog solves it:

1. Place the odor tin tucked just behind a solid corner, baseboard, or sturdy shoebox lid (e.g., inside the open side facing a wall 3–4 inches away) so odor bounces off the barrier and pools slightly to the side.
2. Cue **Search**.
3. Watch your dog investigate the **pool** (he may linger, sniff the floor, or look slightly puzzled). **Do not speak, step closer, or click.**
4. Wait silently as he realizes the concentration drops off and works around the barrier to pinpoint the tin itself.
5. **Click** immediately when his nose reaches true source and reward generously right at the tin.

> [!TIP]
> **Training Tip: Don't Rescue Too Early**
> Handlers love to step in when a dog looks confused at an odor pool. Unless your dog is getting visibly frustrated or about to walk away entirely, give him 10–15 quiet seconds to think. Solving an odor pool independently is a huge confidence booster.

---

### Step 3: Inaccessible hides ("taking the leap").

In real-world searching (and nosework trials), dogs frequently find hides they cannot directly touch.

1. Place the odor tin inside a **closed** breathable container—such as a sturdy Tupperware container with 4–5 small holes poked in the lid, or a closed cardboard shoebox with a small vent hole.
2. Set it on the non-slip mat alongside 1–2 empty closed containers.
3. Cue **Search**.
4. The moment your dog pinpoints the scented container and holds his nose calmly at the vent hole or lid edge _without pawing, biting, or trying to pry it open_, **Click!** and feed him right against the container wall.

> [!NOTE]
> **Preventing Frustration on Inaccessible Hides**
> If your dog starts scratching or mouthing the closed container, make the setup easier: crack the lid slightly or click _earlier_ (after just 0.5 seconds of calm sniffing) before frustration builds. Keep sessions upbeat and low-arousal.

---

### Step 4: Elevated hides (working vertical odor).

Introduce vertical hides so your dog learns to lift his nose and work falling odor cones rather than only scanning the floor:

1. Start easy at **chin or nose height** (resting on a low stool or chair seat), then gradually build up to **head-reach height** (such as a table edge, windowsill, or bookshelf 2–3 feet off the floor).
2. Let a few minutes pass before starting the search so odor settles downward slightly.
3. Cue **Search**. Notice if your dog initially sniffs the pool on the floor underneath the furniture before lifting his nose to pinpoint the higher hide.
4. **Click** when his nose points as close to the elevated tin as he can reach, and bring your treat hand up to feed him right at source.

---

### Step 5: Converging odor (two hides in proximity).

Place **two** odor tins 3–5 feet apart (for example, on opposite sides of a doorway or inside two separate objects in the same area) so their scent cones partially overlap.

1. Cue **Search** and let your dog enter the overlapping scent picture.
2. He may ping-pong slightly between the two plumes before committing to the stronger/closer one. Stay neutral and quiet.
3. When he commits to Source A, **Click** and feed 2–3 treats at Source A, then calmly cover or pick up Tin A and say **Find Another**.
4. Wait for him to clear the lingering scent of Tin A and work cleanly to Source B. **Click** and jackpot at Source B.

#### Comeafters

Take all the skills from Levels 1–3 into novel rooms, porches, garages, vehicles, and outdoor yards—always remembering to start with a few easy reps (_Chutes and Ladders_) whenever you introduce a brand-new environment.
