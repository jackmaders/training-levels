# Nosework Level 2: Discrimination & Commitment to Source

## Comebefores

Complete [Nosework Level 1](./level-1-foundation-and-odor.md) first. Your dog should reliably orient his nose to an **unpaired** target odor tin in a single box without food inside and without handler pointing.

## Where We're Going

- Step 1: Dog discriminates between 2 boxes (1 hot, 1 cold).
- Step 2: Dog selects the single hot box from a 3–4 box lineup or semi-circle.
- Step 3: Dog holds nose at source for 1–2 seconds (building commitment/hover).
- Step 4: Dog transitions off cardboard boxes to household objects.
- Step 5: Dog solves two separate hides in one session (_"Find Another"_).

## Think About Discrimination & Commitment

Once there is more than one container in front of your dog, guessing no longer works—he must **discriminate** between "hot" (contains target odor) and "cold" (blank).

At this stage, many handlers rush to teach a formal alert behavior like a _Sit_ or _Down_ at the hide. However, prematurely drilling sits often makes dogs offer sits on _handler cues_ or guesswork rather than genuine odor commitment. Instead, Stacy Barnett emphasizes **commitment to source**: teaching your dog to freeze or hover his nose firmly at the exact source of the odor while waiting for his payout right at the tin.

> [!WARNING]
> **Avoid Cross-Contamination!**
> Target odor oils transfer easily via fingers, drool, and surfaces. Always handle your "hot" tin with tweezers or a dedicated hand, never touch "cold" decoy boxes with your odor hand, and **never put food inside cold boxes** during discrimination drills. If a box gets drooled on or touched by the hot tin, retire it from the cold pool.

## About the Cues

- **Search:** Said calmly once as you present the lineup or release your dog.
- **Find Another** (introduced in Step 5): Used smoothly after rewarding the first hide to invite your dog to keep hunting for a second source.

## Steps

### Step 1: Two-box discrimination (1 hot, 1 cold).

Set two identical shallow boxes side-by-side about 18–24 inches apart on the floor in front of your dog.

1. Place the unpaired odor tin in **one** box (the hot box) and leave the second box completely empty (the cold box).
2. Sit quietly (_"You Search, I Sit"_ mindset) and say **Search**.
3. If your dog sniffs the cold box, stay silent and motionless—do not say "No" or "Wrong," and do not click. Let him discover on his own that the blank box pays nothing.
4. The instant he shifts to the hot box and puts his nose on the tin, **Click!** and feed 2–3 treats one by one **directly at the tin**.

Pick up both boxes out of sight, swap the hot box to the other side (or use a fresh hot/cold pair so you aren't just moving the same box), and repeat 4–5 times.

> [!NOTE]
> **Problem: My dog keeps sniffing the cold box and looking at me**
> He is testing whether _any_ box pays. Stay completely neutral and wait. If he gives up and stares at you for 10+ seconds, pick both boxes up quietly, reset with a **paired** treat right next to the tin for 1 repetition to remind him what pays, then return to unpaired.

---

### Step 2: Three-to-four box lineup or semi-circle.

Arrange 3 (then 4) identical boxes in a gentle arc or straight line, spaced 18–24 inches apart.

1. Place the hot tin in one box at random (avoid always putting it on the ends or in the middle).
2. Cue **Search** and keep your eyes neutral (look at a spot on the wall or floor rather than staring at the hot box).
3. **Click** only when his nose commits to the tin inside the hot box, and feed multiple treats at source.

#### Try It Cold

Set up a 4-box arc where _you_ don't look at the box placement (or have a family member place the tin while you turn your back). Run the search **blind** so you cannot accidentally telegraph the hot box with your posture or gaze.

---

### Step 3: Building duration at source (the hover / freeze).

We want your dog to stay glued to the odor source rather than brushing past it and snapping his head back toward your treat pouch.

1. Present a 2–4 box setup with 1 hot tin.
2. When your dog reaches the hot tin, **wait just 0.5 seconds** (half a beat) before clicking while his nose stays on the tin.
3. **Click** while his nose is still on source, then deliver 3–4 small treats slowly, one after another, right against the tin ("jackpotting at source").
4. Over several sessions, gradually stretch that pause from 0.5 seconds to 1 second, then 2 seconds—always clicking _before_ he pulls his nose away.

> [!TIP]
> **Training Tip: Reward Timing Shapes Commitment**
> If you click _after_ your dog turns his head toward you, you are rewarding "leaving source." Keep your thumb ready and click **while the nose is still pointed squarely at the tin**.

---

### Step 4: Transitioning off boxes to household objects.

Cardboard boxes are training wheels; real nosework happens in the environment. Place the odor tin on or inside everyday household items alongside 2–3 decoy items:

- Under one of 3 overturned vented plastic cups or heavy ceramic bowls (weight them or hold them if your dog likes to flip things).
- Tucked in the corner of one folded towel among 3 folded towels on the floor.
- Placed behind a table leg, shoe, or cushion in the room.

Start with just 1 decoy item, then build to 3 decoys across a quiet room.

---

### Step 5: Multiple hides in one session ("Find Another").

Teaching your dog that a search does not always end after one find builds stamina and systematic hunting.

1. Place **two** separate hides about 4–6 feet apart in a small search area, along with 2–3 cold decoys.
2. Cue **Search**. When your dog finds the first hide, **Click** and feed 2 treats at source.
3. Instead of ending the session, calmly cover or pick up the first tin, say **Find Another**, and gesture neutrally toward the rest of the area.
4. Wait quietly until he works out the second hide, **Click**, and deliver a big jackpot at the second source to end the game.

#### Comeafters

Keep sessions short (2–3 rounds of 1–2 hides). Gradually spread boxes and household hides across larger rooms and novel indoor spaces while maintaining a confident, systematic search pattern.
