# Cooperative Care Level 2: Equipment, Muzzles & Stations

## Comebefores

Complete [Cooperative Care Level 1](./level-1-consent-and-start-buttons.md). Your dog should understand the **Start/Stop Button** rule via a sustained chin rest or the Bucket Game.

## Where We're Going

- Step 1: Dog pushes nose happily into a basket muzzle (`Muzzle`).
- Step 2: Dog willingly puts head through an E-collar, coat, or harness opening.
- Step 3: Dog targets an oral syringe for liquid medication (`Medicine`).
- Step 4: Dog holds a voluntary four-footed **Stand** during a full-body inspection.
- Step 5: Dog maintains a chin rest during simulated ear and eye drops.

## Think About Equipment & Stations

Even the gentlest dog may someday need a basket muzzle (after an accident when in pain, or to prevent scavenging), an E-collar, liquid antibiotics, or a standing exam on a vet scale. If the very first time your dog sees a muzzle or syringe is during a stressful emergency, the equipment itself becomes scary. Conditioning gear ahead of time turns medical tools into predictable, high-paying games.

## About the Cues

- **Muzzle:** Cues the dog to push her muzzle deeply into the basket.
- **Medicine:** Cues the dog to lick or accept liquid from a plastic oral syringe.
- **Stand:** Cues a calm, balanced four-on-the-floor stand for body checks.

## Steps

### Step 1: The muzzle as a treat cone (`Muzzle`).

Choose a breathable basket muzzle that allows your dog to pant, drink, and take treats easily. (If you don't own one yet, start with a clean, smooth-edged plastic yogurt tub with the bottom cut out.)

1. **Peek Inside:** Hold the muzzle steady in front of you with treats visible inside. When your dog leans in to investigate or eat a treat out of the basket, **Click!** and let her pull her head back out. Never push the muzzle _onto_ her face—let _her_ put her nose inside.
2. **Duration Inside:** Feed 2–3 treats in a row through the front openings of the basket while her nose stays tucked inside, then say **"All done"** and lower the muzzle.
3. **Add the Cue & Straps:** As she eagerly dives her nose in, say **Muzzle**. While she keeps her nose in for treats, gently drape the straps behind her ears (do not buckle yet) $\rightarrow$ **Click** $\rightarrow$ Treat through the basket.
4. **Buckle & Move:** Buckle the strap behind her ears for 2 seconds, feed a treat, unbuckle, and end on a happy note. Gradually build up to wearing it comfortably for 30–60 seconds while walking or resting.

---

### Step 2: E-collar & gear dress-up.

Teach your dog that putting her head through openings predicts great rewards:

1. Hold an open soft E-collar, step-in coat neck, or slip-over harness in front of you at nose height.
2. Hold a treat on the _far_ side of the opening so your dog chooses to poke her nose and ears partway through. **Click** and feed _through_ the cone/collar, letting her back out immediately.
3. Gradually wait until her whole head passes through before clicking, touch the velcro or clasp lightly, feed a treat, and take it right back off.

> [!NOTE]
> **Always Let the Dog Exit Freely**
> Just like crate training, forcing a cone over a dog's ears destroys trust. Let her step into and back out of the cone at her own pace during training reps.

---

### Step 3: Syringe targeting (`Medicine`).

Liquid medications and dewormers are effortless when your dog views a plastic syringe as a flavor dispenser.

1. Fill a clean needleless oral dosing syringe with plain water, dog-safe bone broth, or diluted plain yogurt.
2. Hold the tip of the syringe at nose level. When your dog sniffs or licks the tip, **Click** and squeeze out a tiny drop right onto her tongue.
3. Add the cue **Medicine** as she reaches eagerly for the tip.
4. Practice placing the tip gently at the _side_ corner of her lips (behind the canine teeth, where liquid doesn't shoot straight down the throat), squeezing a small drop of broth, clicking, and following with a tasty chaser treat.

---

### Step 4: Voluntary "Stand" for inspection (`Stand`).

Many dogs automatically sit or lie down when a handler approaches to check them, which makes checking the belly, groin, knees, or tail difficult.

1. **Get the Stand:** From a sit, hold a treat right at nose level and move your hand horizontally straight forward 2–3 inches so your dog rocks up onto all four feet without walking forward. **Click** the instant all four feet are planted, and feed at nose level (keeping her standing).
2. **Add Duration & Cue:** Say **Stand** as she rises. Pause 2–3 seconds before clicking and feeding.
3. **Hands-On Inspection:** While she holds a **Stand** (or rests her chin in your left hand while standing), run your right hand smoothly along her spine, ribcage, chest, and hips. Click and feed for staying balanced on all four feet.

#### Try It Cold

Have a helper stand beside you while your dog holds a **Stand** on a non-slip mat and gently run their hands over her back and flanks for 3 seconds.

---

### Step 5: Ear and eye drop simulation.

Use the **Chin Rest** from Level 1 so your dog's head stays still and tilted comfortably:

1. Ask for **Chin** (on your palm or rolled towel).
2. Bring a clean, empty dropper bottle into view 6 inches from her eye or ear $\rightarrow$ **Click** $\rightarrow$ Treat while chin stays down.
3. Bring the bottle within 1 inch of the ear opening or above the eye (never touching the eyeball!) $\rightarrow$ **Click** $\rightarrow$ Jackpot.
4. Gently tilt her ear flap open and squeeze a tiny drop of plain lukewarm saline (for ear practice) or touch the plastic bottle tip softly to the fur near the inner corner of the eye (for eye drops) $\rightarrow$ **Click** $\rightarrow$ Big reward.

#### Comeafters

Keep dropper simulations brief (2–3 reps per session) and always respect her **Stop Button** if she lifts her chin.
