# Cooperative Care Levels — Steps to Success

_A force-free, step-by-step Side Quest building on Main Quest Handling to turn grooming, medication, and veterinary care into voluntary teamwork._

## How This Side Quest Connects to the Main Quest

In the Main Quest ([Level 2 Handling](../../markdown/level-2/handling.md) and [Level 3 Handling](../../markdown/level-3/handling.md)), your dog learns to remain calm and relaxed while you or a vet examine her body. Cooperative Care takes that trust one step further by giving your dog an explicit **consent signal ("start/stop button")**.

Instead of simply enduring nail trims, ear drops, or injections, your dog learns:

1. **How to say "Yes, I'm ready":** Holding a **Chin Rest** or looking steadily at a treat bucket signals that you may proceed.
2. **How to say "Pause, I need a break":** Lifting her chin or looking away immediately stops the procedure—no grabbing, pinning, or forcing—so she never feels trapped or needs to escalate to growling or snapping.
3. **Predictable Husbandry Routines:** Equipment like basket muzzles, E-collars, syringes, and scratchboards become fun, well-paid puzzles long before an emergency ever arrives.

> [!TIP]
> **Why Practice This Before You Need It?**
> Conditioning cooperative care when your dog is healthy and relaxed builds deep emotional resilience. When an actual vet visit, ear infection, or nail trim comes along, you already have a shared language.

- [Level 2: Equipment, Muzzles & Stations](level-2-equipment-and-stations.md) — Basket muzzle conditioning, E-collar/gear dress-up, syringe targeting, voluntary **Stand** for inspection, and ear/eye drop simulations.
