# Cooperative Care Level 1: Consent & Start Buttons

## Comebefores

Complete [Level 1 Target](../../markdown/level-1/target.md) and [Level 1 Zen](../../markdown/level-1/zen.md) in the Main Quest. Your dog should understand how a click/marker predicts a treat and be comfortable working calmly near your hands.

## Where We're Going

- Step 1: Dog places chin flat onto your palm or a rolled towel (`Chin`).
- Step 2: Dog holds the chin rest for 3–5 seconds.
- Step 3: Dog learns the **Start/Stop Button** rule while your other hand reaches to touch.
- Step 4: Dog plays the **Bucket Game** (visual consent station).
- Step 5: Dog maintains consent during everyday checks (ears, eyes, teeth, collar).

## Equipment

You need small, soft treats, your marker (clicker or **"Yes!"**), a rolled towel or cushion (optional station for your dog's chin), and a clean plastic tub or bowl for the Bucket Game.

## Think About Consent & Start Buttons

Most dogs tolerate routine care until something unexpectedly pinches, stings, or scares them—and then they learn to duck away or fight back. When we hold a dog still against her will, we teach her that mild protests don't work, which can push a sensitive dog straight to snapping.

A **Start/Stop Button** flips the script:

- **Start Button (Chin Down / Stare at Bucket):** _"I am ready. You may touch or treat me."_
- **Stop Button (Chin Up / Look Away):** _"Stop right now and reset."_

Because your dog knows she is always in control of pausing the session, her anxiety drops dramatically—and paradoxically, she will hold still _longer_ than a dog who feels restrained.

## About the Cues

- **Chin:** Added once your dog eagerly rests her chin weight into your flat palm or onto a rolled towel.
- **Ready (for the Bucket Game):** Setting the bucket down at eye level acts as the visual invitation.

## Steps

### Step 1: Capturing and shaping the chin target (`Chin`).

Sit comfortably beside your dog while she is sitting or lying down. Hold your non-treating hand flat, palm facing up, about an inch below her chin.

1. **Lure or Capture:** Hold a tiny treat just past your fingertips so her chin naturally lowers to brush your palm as she reaches for it. **Click** the instant her chin skin touches your palm, and feed from the other hand.
2. Fade the lure quickly: after 3–4 reps, offer your flat empty palm below her chin and wait. The instant she drops her chin onto your hand, **Click** and treat right there.
3. Repeat 5–8 times until she actively seeks out your palm with her chin.

> [!TIP]
> **Optional Towel Station**
> Some dogs prefer resting their chin on a rolled towel on a sofa arm, coffee table, or your lap rather than an open hand (which frees up both of your hands for grooming later). Simply place your flat palm on top of the rolled towel first, then fade your hand out so she rests her chin directly on the towel.

---

### Step 2: Building duration (3–5 second chin rest).

Once your dog plants her chin reliably, begin waiting a split second before clicking so she learns to rest her _weight_ calmly in your hand.

1. Ask for **Chin**. When her chin lands, pause 1 second before clicking, and feed her _while her chin stays on your palm_ (or immediately re-offer your palm).
2. Gradually build to 2 seconds, 3 seconds, and then 5 full seconds of relaxed chin weight.
3. Notice her neck and jaw relaxing—click soft, heavy chin rests rather than tense hovering.

#### Try It Cold

Offer your open palm (or rolled towel) in a different room without any warm-up reps. Say **Chin** and see if she smoothly rests her chin for 3 seconds.

---

### Step 3: Introducing the "Start/Stop Button" rule.

Now teach your dog what the chin rest actually controls: your other hand moving toward her body.

1. Cue **Chin**. While she holds her chin down, slowly move your _free_ hand 6 inches toward her shoulder—and stop before touching. If her chin stayed down, **Click** and treat!
2. Next rep: reach all the way in and rest your fingers lightly on her shoulder or collar for 1 second. Chin still down? **Click** and jackpot!
3. **The Golden Rule (The Stop Button):** If at _any_ moment she lifts her chin or turns her head away while you reach, **immediately freeze and pull your touching hand back to neutral**. Do not scold, sigh, or withhold the next chance. Wait 2 seconds, offer your palm again, and make the next rep easier (shorter reach, lighter touch).

---

### Step 4: The Bucket Game (visual consent station).

Popularized by Chirag Patel, the **Bucket Game** uses eye focus on a bowl instead of a chin rest—ideal when you need both hands free to brush, dry paws, or apply topical wipes.

1. Hold or set a small tub/bucket filled with treats at your dog's nose/eye level, slightly to one side.
2. Wait silently until your dog looks steadily at the bucket. Say **"Yes!"** (or click), take a treat from the bucket, and feed her. Repeat 4–5 times so staring at the bucket becomes a calm laser-focus behavior.
3. While she stares at the bucket, reach smoothly with your other hand to touch her flank, paw, or ear.
   - **If she stays locked on the bucket:** Mark **"Yes!"** and feed from the bucket.
   - **If she looks away from the bucket toward your hand:** Pause your touching hand immediately, wait for her to look back at the bucket, and try an easier touch.

---

### Step 5: Applying consent to everyday checks (ears, eyes, teeth, collar).

Use either **Chin** or the **Bucket Game** to practice brief, gentle health checks:

- Lifting an ear flap gently for 1 second $\rightarrow$ **Click** $\rightarrow$ Treat.
- Parting the upper lip briefly to look at a canine tooth $\rightarrow$ **Click** $\rightarrow$ Treat.
- Wiping gently under an eye with a damp cotton pad $\rightarrow$ **Click** $\rightarrow$ Treat.
- Sliding two fingers under her collar as if clipping a leash $\rightarrow$ **Click** $\rightarrow$ Treat.

#### Comeafters

Practice 2-minute consent sessions in different rooms and with another family member playing the "checker" while you hold the chin station or bucket.
