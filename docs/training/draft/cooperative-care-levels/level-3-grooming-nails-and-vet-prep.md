# Cooperative Care Level 3: Scratchboard, Nails & Vet Prep

## Comebefores

Complete [Cooperative Care Levels 1](./level-1-consent-and-start-buttons.md) and [2](./level-2-equipment-and-stations.md), as well as [Level 2 Target](../../markdown/level-2/target.md) (which teaches paw targeting) and [Level 3 Handling](../../markdown/level-3/handling.md).

## Where We're Going

- Step 1: Dog files her own front nails on a scratchboard (`Scratch`).
- Step 2: Dog offers a calm, sustained paw hold for nail clippers or a grinder (`Paw`).
- Step 3: Dog maintains consent during brushing and combing of sensitive areas.
- Step 4: Dog holds a chin rest through faux-injection and blood-draw holds (`Poke`).
- Step 5: Dog completes a full 2-minute mock nose-to-tail exam with a helper.

## Think About Nail Care & Vet Prep

Nail trims and veterinary pokes are the two biggest triggers for clinic stress. A **scratchboard** removes the pressure from front nail care by turning maintenance into an active paw-targeting game, while **desensitizing clippers, pinches, and leg holds** at home ensures your dog has hundreds of positive repetitions banked before a vet ever picks up a syringe.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Always Put the Board Away Between Sessions!**
> A scratchboard is a tool, not a chew toy or unsupervised floor mat. Put it out of sight when the 2-minute session ends so your dog doesn't over-file her nails down to the quick or scratch doors and furniture.

## About the Cues

- **Scratch:** Cues a firm downward paw swipe on the sandpaper board.
- **Paw:** Cues placing and resting a paw calmly into your hand.
- **Poke:** A gentle warning cue spoken right before a mild skin pinch or touch, so nothing ever surprises your dog.

## Steps

### Step 1: Targeting a scratchboard (`Scratch`).

Make or buy a sturdy wooden scratchboard covered in medium-grit (80–100 grit) sandpaper. Start with the board resting flat or at a gentle 30–45 degree angle against your shins or a wall.

1. **Touch the Board:** Place a sticky note or treat briefly behind/near the board, or wait for your dog to investigate. Click any single front-paw tap on the sandpaper and reward _away_ from the board so she resets.
2. **Drag Downward:** Hold the board at a 45-degree angle. Click only when her paw makes a slight downward **scratch** motion against the grit rather than a light tap.
3. **Add the Cue & Alternate Paws:** Cue **Scratch** as she lifts her paw. Run 3–5 swipes per paw (checking nail tips frequently so you never sand too far), then put the board away.

---

### Step 2: Sustained paw hold for clippers or grinder (`Paw`).

Even with a scratchboard, dewclaws and back nails need gentle trimming or grinding.

1. **Voluntary Paw Offer:** Hold your open palm low in front of your dog (sitting or lying comfortably). Click when she places her paw in your hand, and build to 3–5 seconds of relaxed paw weight while your thumb gently touches individual toes.
2. **Introduce the Tool:** Set silent clippers (or an off-switch nail grinder) beside your dog $\rightarrow$ **Click** $\rightarrow$ Treat. Turn the grinder on for 1 second across the room $\rightarrow$ **Click** $\rightarrow$ Treat.
3. **Combine Consent + Touch:** Use a **Chin Rest** or the **Bucket Game** while holding one paw:
   - Touch the closed clipper handle (or vibrating back of the grinder) to one nail for half a second $\rightarrow$ **Click** $\rightarrow$ Jackpot!
   - Clip a tiny dry piece of uncooked spaghetti tucked beside her nail so she hears the familiar _snap_ without any pressure on her toe $\rightarrow$ **Click** $\rightarrow$ Treat.
   - Trim **one** nail tip per session at first, ending cleanly while she is still relaxed.

---

### Step 3: Brushing and line-combing with consent.

Matting and tugging make dogs dread brushes. Use the **Bucket Game** (from Level 1) so your dog controls the pace:

1. Show the brush, let your dog lock eyes on the treat bucket, and make one gentle stroke down her back (an easy, non-sensitive area) $\rightarrow$ Mark **"Yes!"** and feed from the bucket.
2. Work slowly toward trickier zones—ears, leg feathers, belly, tail, and paws—taking 1–2 gentle strokes at a time.
3. Anytime she looks away from the bucket, pause brushing immediately and resume only when she looks back at the bucket.

---

### Step 4: Faux-injection and blood-draw prep (`Poke`).

Warning cues prevent startle responses during vaccines and blood draws:

1. **The Scruff Pinch (`Poke`):** Ask for **Chin**. Say **"Poke"** in a calm, matter-of-fact tone, gently tent/pinch the loose skin at the back of her neck or shoulder for 1 second, **Click**, and deliver a jackpot while she holds her chin down. Build gradually to a mild fingernail tap (simulating a needle prick) paired with **Poke**.
2. **The Foreleg Hold:** While your dog sits or lies down holding a chin rest (or looking at the treat bucket), calmly wrap your fingers around her forearm just below the elbow (where a vet tech holds off a vein), hold gently for 3 seconds, tap the inside of the leg lightly with a blunt pen cap, **Click**, and feed multiple treats.
3. Practice wrapping a soft elastic gauze band loosely around the forearm for 5 seconds while she eats from a lick mat or holds a chin rest.

---

### Step 5: Full mock exam with a helper.

Recruit a friend or family member to act as the "veterinarian" while your dog works on a non-slip mat or low grooming table:

1. Cue **Chin** (or **Stand** / **Settle**) and have the helper approach calmly from the side.
2. Over 1–2 minutes, the helper performs a complete nose-to-tail routine in short 2-second pieces, with a click and treat after each element:
   - Look in both ears and eyes; lift upper lip to check teeth.
   - Run hands down neck, chest (mimic stethoscope touch), and belly.
   - Lift each paw briefly and touch the tail.
   - Say **"Poke"** and gently tent skin at the shoulder.
3. End with a cheerful release (**OK!**) and a play or sniff break.

#### Try It Cold

Visit your actual veterinary clinic for a quick **"Happy Visit"**—step onto the scale, practice a 10-second Chin Rest in the lobby or exam room, collect treats from a friendly vet tech, and head straight back home with zero medical procedures.
