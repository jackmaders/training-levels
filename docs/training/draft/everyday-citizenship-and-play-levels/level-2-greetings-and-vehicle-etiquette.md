# Citizenship Level 2: Controlled Greetings & Vehicle Control

## Comebefores

Complete [Citizenship Level 1](./level-1-structured-play-and-thresholds.md), plus [Level 2 Lazy Leash](../../markdown/level-2/lazy-leash.md) and [Level 2 Come](../../markdown/level-2/come.md) in the Main Quest.

## Where We're Going

- Step 1: Dog approaches a stranger on a loose lead and pauses politely without jumping (`Say Hello`).
- Step 2: Dog maintains four paws on the floor (or a sit) while a stranger pets her.
- Step 3: Dog completes a **Controlled Greeting** alongside another dog and handler.
- Step 4: Dog enters a vehicle calmly on cue (`Load Up`).
- Step 5: Dog waits inside an open car door/boot until clipped and released (`Out`).

## Think About Greetings & Vehicles

Jumping up on friendly strangers and launching out of an open car boot are two of the most common everyday manners struggles—and both are directly tested in the Kennel Club Silver Award.

The secret to polite greetings is **taking the pressure off the dog**: keep approaches short (3–5 seconds at first), pay heavily _on the floor_ (dropping treats between her paws discourages leaping upward), and never force an uncertain dog to accept petting from strangers.

For vehicles, treat the car door/boot sill exactly like a front door threshold: **an open door means "Wait," never "Launch!"**

## About the Cues

- **Say Hello:** Permission to calmly greet a person once you have stopped together.
- **Load Up (or Hup):** Cue to jump calmly into the car boot or back seat (or place front paws up for smaller/older dogs to be lifted).
- **Out:** Release cue to exit the car safely once the lead is secured.

## Steps

### Step 1: Approaching and stopping for a stranger (`Say Hello`).

Recruit a helper to stand quietly 10 feet away with hands resting at their sides.

1. Walk calmly toward the helper on a loose lead, stopping 4–5 feet away (just outside jumping range).
2. Wait silently for your dog to sit or stand calmly with all four feet on the floor $\rightarrow$ **Click** and drop a treat right between her front paws.
3. Have your helper take one step closer and say _"Hello!"_ cheerfully without reaching toward your dog. Keep feeding a treat or two on the floor if needed so your dog stays grounded while you shake hands or chat for 5 seconds.

---

### Step 2: Controlled greeting while being petted.

Now add brief, polite physical greeting:

1. Approach and stop 3 feet from your helper. Ask your dog to **Sit** (or hold a calm stand).
2. Say **Say Hello**, and have the helper calmly reach out to scratch her chest or shoulder (avoid reaching straight over the top of the head) for 2 seconds.
3. If all four paws remain on the floor, **Click** and reward _from your hand or on the floor_ (not the stranger's hand—so your dog doesn't mob strangers' pockets!).
4. If your dog starts to jump, calmly step backward 2 paces with the lead to reset, ask for a sit again, and try a shorter 1-second pet.

> [!TIP]
> **What If My Dog Is Shy Around Strangers?**
> Never force a cautious dog to be petted. Use the _"Say Hello from 4 feet away"_ version (Step 1), letting your dog sniff the air and look back to you for treats while you chat with the person. Comfort comes before contact.

---

### Step 3: Controlled greeting with another dog present.

This mirrors the classic **KC Silver Controlled Greeting** test:

1. Arrange with a friend who has a calm, polite dog to walk toward each other on parallel paths, stopping 6–8 feet apart so the two dogs do not tangle leads.
2. Ask your dog to sit or settle beside you while you and the other handler step closer enough to exchange a brief greeting and handshake.
3. Click and feed calmly for ignoring the other dog, keeping a loose lead, and checking back in with you.
4. Keep the chat to 10–15 seconds at first, then praise cheerfully and walk calmly away together on a loose lead.

---

### Step 4: Controlled vehicle entry (`Load Up`).

Practice next to a parked car in a quiet driveway (if your dog is small or shouldn't jump into tall SUVs, use a ramp or teach a calm front-paws-up boost).

1. Walk to the closed car door or boot on a loose lead and pause (`Wait`).
2. Open the door/boot smoothly. Your dog should remain standing or sitting outside on the ground until invited.
3. Gesture into the car/crate and say **Load Up**.
4. **Click** the moment she steps/hops cleanly onto the seat or into the travel crate, deliver 2–3 high-value treats inside the car, and attach her seatbelt harness or close the travel crate door.

---

### Step 5: Open-door vehicle wait and controlled exit (`Out`).

Never allow a dog to dive straight out of a car onto a driveway or road:

1. With the car parked safely and your dog inside (harnessed or in her travel crate), unclip/open the crate or car door **just 3 inches**. If she pushes forward, smoothly close it 3 inches without scolding.
2. Open the door/boot fully while keeping one hand casually ready near her collar/lead. Count "1, 2, 3" silently. If she stays inside calmly, **Click** and deliver a treat _inside the car_.
3. Clip the lead calmly onto her collar/harness, step back slightly to give clear landing room, and say **Out**!
4. Guide her cleanly out onto the ground (never let small dogs leap from high trunks—lift gently on your cue) and reward her for standing calmly beside you on a loose lead.

#### Try It Cold

Drive to a quiet, unfamiliar park carpark. Run the full **Step 5** open-door wait and **Out** routine with real outdoor smells drifting into the open boot before beginning your walk.
