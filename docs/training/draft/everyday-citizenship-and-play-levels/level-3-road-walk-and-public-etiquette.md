# Citizenship Level 3: Road Walk, Kerbs & Public Etiquette

## Comebefores

Complete [Citizenship Levels 1](./level-1-structured-play-and-thresholds.md) and [2](./level-2-greetings-and-vehicle-etiquette.md), plus [Level 3 Lazy Leash](../../markdown/level-3/lazy-leash.md) and [Level 3 Go To Mat](../../markdown/level-3/go-to-mat.md) in the Main Quest.

## Where We're Going

- Step 1: Dog automatically stops/pauses at the kerb edge when you stop.
- Step 2: Dog crosses the road briskly and calmly beside you (`Cross`).
- Step 3: Dog walks on the safe (inside) side of the pavement (`Side`) around obstacles.
- Step 4: Dog completes a 3–5 minute **Road Walk** past traffic, joggers, and cyclists.
- Step 5: Dog settles calmly under a public bench or café table around activity.

## Think About Roadcraft & Public Etiquette

Both the Silver and Gold Kennel Club awards include a **Road Walk**—because loose-lead walking in a quiet field is very different from navigating kerbs, parked cars, wheelie bins, passing cyclists, and noisy buses.

Consistent kerb habits protect your dog's life if a lead ever slips from your hand. Just like doorways, **every kerb is a natural stop sign**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Safety First Near Traffic**
> Always train roadside skills on a short, secure 4–6 foot flat lead (never a long line or extendable lead near vehicles). Start Steps 1–3 on a quiet cul-de-sac, driveway apron, or painted parking-lot line before moving onto a busy street.

## About the Cues

- **Wait (or Kerb Stop):** Automatic pause when you halt at the kerb edge.
- **Cross:** Crisp cue to walk straight across the road beside you without sniffing the tarmac.
- **Side (or Left/Right):** Optional cue to position your dog on the side farthest from passing cars.

## Steps

### Step 1: Stopping at the kerb edge.

Start on a quiet driveway or footpath with a clear kerb line.

1. Walk toward the kerb on a loose lead. Halt smoothly 1 foot back from the drop-off.
2. The instant your dog stops moving forward with you (and especially if she offers a sit or looks back at you), **Click** and feed right beside your leg so she stays behind the kerb line.
3. If she steps off the kerb before you, take one quiet step backward to reset her onto the pavement, wait for slack in the lead, and try again.
4. Repeat at 5–6 different kerbs until halting at the edge becomes automatic without needing a verbal **Sit** or **Wait**.

---

### Step 2: Crossing the road under control (`Cross`).

Make crossing streets brisk, focused, and non-negotiable (no dawdling or sniffing mid-crosswalk):

1. Stop cleanly at the kerb (Step 1). Check both ways for traffic.
2. When clear, say **Cross** in an upbeat tone and step off together at a purposeful walking pace.
3. Click/praise verbally (_"Good cross!"_) mid-stride if she stays focused beside you rather than putting her nose down on the asphalt.
4. Immediately deliver a treat jackpot the moment both of you step safely onto the far pavement.

---

### Step 3: Walking on the safe (inside) side (`Side`).

If you walk near moving traffic, keeping your dog between you and the buildings/hedge (rather than right next to the road gutter) gives an extra margin of safety.

1. Decide whether your dog is on your left or right depending on the direction of traffic.
2. Use a hand target or smooth U-turn to guide her to your inside leg if she drifts toward the kerb, clicking and feeding right at your hip seam.
3. Practice navigating narrow pavement pinch-points—walking smoothly single-file past a parked car, lamppost, or trash bin without letting the lead snag or tighten.

---

### Step 4: Road walk past traffic, joggers, and cyclists.

Put Steps 1–3 together on a real pavement beside moderate traffic (mirroring **KC Silver Exercise 2** and **Gold Exercise 1**):

1. Walk 3–5 minutes along a public pavement incorporating at least 2 kerb stops, 1–2 road crossings, and everyday distractions (passing cars, cyclists, pedestrians, prams).
2. Whenever an intense distraction (like a noisy lorry or passing jogger) approaches, pay proactively for calm check-ins or keeping the leash loose _before_ your dog reacts or lunges.
3. Keep your pace natural and relaxed—just as taught in [Level 4 Lazy Leash](../../markdown/level-4/lazy-leash.md).

---

### Step 5: Public bench or café settle around activity.

Finish your walk by practicing stillness in public:

1. Bring a small travel mat (or your jacket, per [Level 4 Go To Mat](../../markdown/level-4/go-to-mat.md)) to a quiet outdoor park bench or dog-friendly café patio.
2. Cue **Go To Mat** and **Settle** tucked safely out of the walkway under the bench or beside your chair.
3. Reward calm stillness periodically while you sit quietly for 2–5 minutes as people, strollers, and occasional dogs walk past at a comfortable distance.

#### Try It Cold

Combine a complete mini-outing: **Load Up** into the car, drive to a local high street or park café, wait cleanly on **Out**, walk politely down the pavement with **Kerb Stops** and a **Cross**, enjoy a 5-minute **Café Settle**, and greet one polite friend on the way back to the car.
