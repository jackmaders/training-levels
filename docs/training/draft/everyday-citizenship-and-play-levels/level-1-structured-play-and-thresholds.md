# Citizenship Level 1: Structured Play & Doorway Thresholds

## Comebefores

Complete [Level 1 Zen](../../markdown/level-1/zen.md), [Level 1 Sit](../../markdown/level-1/sit.md), and [Level 2 Relax](../../markdown/level-2/relax.md) in the Main Quest. Your dog should already know how to wait briefly by an open house door and show restraint around food.

## Where We're Going

- Step 1: Dog engages enthusiastically in structured toy play on cue (`Get It`).
- Step 2: Dog releases the toy cleanly for a treat or immediate re-bite (`Thank You`).
- Step 3: Dog cycles smoothly through the **Arousal Switch** (Play $\rightarrow$ `Settle` $\rightarrow$ Play).
- Step 4: Dog holds a formal **Threshold Wait** while you open and pass through doors and gates (`Wait`).
- Step 5: Dog stands or sits calmly while you attach and remove her collar and lead.

## Think About Structured Play & Thresholds

In the Kennel Club Silver Award, handlers are asked to demonstrate constructive play with their dog—not just tossing a ball away, but playing _together_ safely and joyfully. Good toy play gives you a high-value reward you can carry anywhere, while **the Arousal Switch** guarantees you can bring your dog right back down to calm focus afterward.

Similarly, thresholds (front doors, garden gates, car doors) are where excitement usually spikes. Teaching a default pause at every threshold keeps your dog safe near roads and makes outings calm from the very first step.

## About the Cues

- **Get It (or Take It):** Invites your dog to grab and play with the toy.
- **Thank You:** Cues releasing the toy cleanly into your hand.
- **Settle:** Ends the active play burst and asks for a calm breath/rest.
- **Wait:** Hold position behind a doorway or gate until released with **OK** (or **Let's Go**).

## Steps

### Step 1: Constructive toy engagement (`Get It`).

Choose a soft, long tug toy (so hands stay well clear of teeth) or a favorite squeaky plush.

1. Hold the toy still near your hip. Wait for polite eye contact or mild stillness (no leaping at your hands).
2. Sweep the toy playfully across the floor away from your legs, say **Get It**, and let your dog chase and bite it!
3. Tug gently side-to-side at chest/shoulder height (keeping her neck natural—never jerking upward), praising warmly for 5–10 seconds.

> [!NOTE]
> **If Your Dog Isn't Toy-Motivated Yet**
> Keep sessions to 20 seconds and stop _while she still wants more_. Let her "win" the toy occasionally, or rub a smear of cheese on a fleece tug for a couple of starter reps to spark curiosity.

---

### Step 2: Clean toy release and re-engagement (`Thank You`).

Dogs refuse to drop toys when dropping always ends the game. Make letting go the fastest way to keep playing!

1. Freeze your hands completely and pull the toy slightly against your body so it goes "dead" and boring.
2. Say **Thank You** calmly once. Hold a smelly treat right under her nose (or wait quietly 2–3 seconds if she already drops reliably).
3. The instant her jaws open and the toy drops into your hand, **Click** (or say **"Yes!"**), feed the treat, pause 1 second—then immediately sweep the toy out and say **Get It!** to restart the game!
4. Fade out the food trade quickly so the reward for **Thank You** is usually _another round of play_.

---

### Step 3: The Arousal Switch (Play $\rightarrow$ `Settle` $\rightarrow$ Play).

Teach your dog that high excitement and quiet stillness sit right next to each other:

1. Play enthusiastically for 15 seconds.
2. Cue **Thank You** to take the toy, tuck it neatly behind your back, and wait quietly without prompting.
3. Ask for (or capture) a **Sit** or **Down** and wait for one slow exhale or softening of her ears (**Settle**).
4. **Click**, pause 2 seconds, and immediately reward by unleashing another 15-second round of **Get It!**
5. Gradually build the calm **Settle** window from 3 seconds to 15 seconds between play rounds.

---

### Step 4: Doorway & gate threshold default (`Wait`).

Expand [Level 1 Sit](../../markdown/level-1/sit.md) Step 4 into a polished bronze-level doorway and gate routine:

1. Walk up to a closed interior door or garden gate on a loose lead. Stop 2 feet back.
2. Say **Wait** (or simply wait for a default sit/pause). Turn the handle and crack the door 2 inches. If she leans forward, gently close the door without speaking and reset.
3. Open the door fully, step _through_ the threshold yourself while holding the loose lead, and pause on the other side for 2 seconds while she stays behind the doorway line.
4. Say **OK** (or **Let's Go**) and walk her smoothly through beside you, clicking and rewarding once she clears the frame without bolting.

#### Try It Cold

Practice at a brand-new garden gate, patio slider, or training-hall door. Verify she waits calmly until you step through and release her.

---

### Step 5: Calm collar and lead attachment at the threshold.

Before every walk (and for KC Bronze Exercise 2), make gearing up completely calm:

1. Hold the lead and collar calmly while your dog sits or stands quietly. If she bounces or mouths the clip, stand straight and pause until four paws return to the floor.
2. Touch her collar gently $\rightarrow$ **Click** $\rightarrow$ Treat.
3. Clip the lead onto the collar ring (or slip the collar smoothly over her head) while she remains calm $\rightarrow$ **Click** $\rightarrow$ Treat.
4. Practice unclipping the lead just as calmly at the end of a walk _before_ releasing her into the house.

#### Comeafters

Combine Steps 3, 4, and 5: play 15 seconds indoors, **Thank You** + **Settle**, calmly clip the lead on, walk to the front door, **Wait** while you open it, and step out onto a relaxed, loose lead.
