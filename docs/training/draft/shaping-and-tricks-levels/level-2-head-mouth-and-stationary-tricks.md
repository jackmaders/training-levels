# Shaping Lab Level 2: Stationary, Head & Precision Tricks

## Comebefores

Complete [Shaping Lab Level 1](./level-1-the-creativity-game.md) and [Level 2 Target](../../markdown/level-2/target.md). Your dog should be comfortable offering subtle behaviors without needing big physical movements.

## Where We're Going

- Step 1: Dog crosses one front paw over the other while lying down (`Cross`).
- Step 2: Dog tucks nose between front paws or wipes a paw over muzzle (`Shy`).
- Step 3: Dog dips nose into shallow water and blows bubbles (`Bubbles`).
- Step 4: Dog holds a soft object calmly while resting chin (`Keep It`).
- Step 5: Dog offers a captured head tilt or silent "whisper" micro-expression.

## Think About Fine-Motor Trick Shaping

Big, flashy tricks like spinning or jumping are easy for humans to see—which means trainers often overlook how rewarding **tiny, quiet precision tricks** are for dogs. Crossing paws, blowing nose bubbles, and holding objects gently require intense proprioception (awareness of toes, whiskers, and breath) while keeping the dog relaxed in one spot.

Because these movements are subtle, **split your criteria small** and deliver treats calmly right where the dog is lying down so she doesn't jump up between clicks.

## About the Cues

Remember the _Training Levels_ rule from [Level 2 Tricks](../../markdown/level-2/tricks.md): **never name the trick until the dog offers the finished behavior reliably!** Waiting to add cues lets you pick words that fit your dog's exact style (e.g., _Cross_, _Shy_, _Prayers_, _Bubbles_, _Keep It_).

## Steps

### Step 1: Cross paws (`Cross`).

Work with your dog lying comfortably in a sphinx **Down** on a non-slip rug.

1. **Get a Weight Shift or Paw Lift:** Use a tiny target coaster/card placed 2 inches in front of her left paw, or touch the outside of her right wrist lightly so she lifts her right paw an inch. **Click** and feed right between her paws.
2. **Move Across the Midline:** Slide the target card slightly toward the _front-left_ of her left wrist so when she reaches for it, her right paw travels inward across her left leg. **Click** any inward movement!
3. **Hold the Cross:** Wait until her right paw actually touches or rests across her left paw before clicking. Gradually pause 1–2 seconds before clicking so she holds the crossed pose cleanly, then add the cue **Cross** and fade out the coaster.

> [!TIP]
> **Which Paw Is Dominant?**
> Just like humans, dogs usually prefer crossing one specific paw on top. Let her use whichever paw she offers more naturally first; you can teach the opposite side later with a different cue (e.g., _Cross_ vs. _Switch_)!

---

### Step 2: "Say Your Prayers" or "Shy" (`Shy`).

Pick whichever version suits your dog's natural body habits:

- **Option A — Chin Tuck ("Prayers"):** From a down, shape her to lower her nose down _between_ her elbows/wrists toward the floor. You can place a small sticky-note target between her elbows on the mat, click downward nose dips, then fade the target until she bows her head neatly between her front legs.
- **Option B — Paw-Over-Nose ("Shy"):** Place a tiny, dog-safe piece of low-tack painter's tape or post-it lightly on the bridge of her muzzle (only if she finds it mildly curious, never scary!). The split-second she raises a paw to wipe at her nose, **Click!** and immediately remove the tape and reward. After 4–5 reps, remove the tape permanently and capture/shape the paw wipe with your hand gesture or **Shy** cue.

---

### Step 3: Blowing bubbles (`Bubbles`).

Many water-loving dogs discover this naturally; with a shallow bowl, any dog can learn it cleanly:

1. Place a very shallow, wide bowl (a pie tin or wide ceramic dish) on a towel with just **half an inch of water** and 2–3 sinking treats (like small bits of cheese or kibble) at the bottom.
2. Click your dog for dipping her nose into the water to retrieve a treat.
3. Next, use an empty bowl with half an inch of water and **no treats inside**. Wait for her to dip her nose toward the water surface. Dogs naturally exhale out of their noses to keep water out when submerging their nostrils!
4. The instant you see a tiny ripple or bubble in the water, **Click!** and feed a high-value treat dry from your hand. Add **Bubbles** once she dips and blows reliably.

> [!NOTE]
> **Keep Water Depth Shallow**
> Never force a dog's nose into water. Half an inch of lukewarm water in a wide dish lets her keep complete control and breathe comfortably the moment she lifts her muzzle.

---

### Step 4: Chin-down object hold (`Keep It`).

Building on [Level 3 Retrieve](../../markdown/level-3/retrieve.md) (or starting fresh with a soft plush toy/rolled sock):

1. Offer a soft toy at nose level while your dog is lying down. Click for opening her mouth around it, then holding it calmly for 1 second without chomping.
2. Hold your flat palm (or place a soft folded towel) just beneath the toy as she holds it, inviting her to lower her chin onto your hand/towel _while keeping her mouth closed on the toy_.
3. **Click** the instant her chin touches down with the toy still held softly, then take it on **Thank You** and reward. Build up to a 3-second peaceful hold!

---

### Step 5: Micro-expression capture (head tilt or silent "whisper").

Keep your clicker handy during calm downtime to capture charming natural expressions:

- **Head Tilt (`Huh?` / `Really?`):** Play a gentle novel sound (a quiet squeak, a soft trill, or a weird whistle) that makes your dog cock her head to one side. **Click** the head tilt instantly and toss a treat! Repeat with the same quiet vocal cue until she tilts on command.
- **Silent "Whisper" / Cheek Puff:** If your dog ever makes a tiny quiet _boof_ or mouth-flap before barking, capture that soft closed-mouth puff with a click and calm treat, fading out loud barks completely.

#### Try It Cold

Ask for two of your Level 2 stationary tricks in a row (for example: **Cross**, treat, **Shy**, treat) in a brand-new room to test how cleanly your dog distinguishes the cues.
