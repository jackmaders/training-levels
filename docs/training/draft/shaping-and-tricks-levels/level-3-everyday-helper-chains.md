# Shaping Lab Level 3: Everyday Helper Chains

## Comebefores

Complete [Shaping Lab Levels 1](./level-1-the-creativity-game.md) and [2](./level-2-head-mouth-and-stationary-tricks.md), plus [Level 1 Target](../../markdown/level-1/target.md) and [Level 3 Retrieve](../../markdown/level-3/retrieve.md) (helpful for Step 4).

## Where We're Going

- Step 1: Dog pushes a cabinet door or drawer closed with her nose (`Shut It`).
- Step 2: Dog presses a push-light, service bell, or talking button (`Button`).
- Step 3: Dog pulls a fleece tug/towel to open a drawer or slide a basket (`Pull`).
- Step 4: Dog tidies toys into a basket (`Tidy Up`).
- Step 5: Dog links a 3-part behavior chain from start to finish before the click.

## Think About Everyday Helper Chains

Once your dog knows how to target, hold, push, and pull, you can link simple pieces together into useful, impressive **helper routines**.

The secret to clean multi-step behaviors is **back-chaining**: always teach the _last_ step of the chain first (where the reward happens) so your dog finishes strong instead of stalling out halfway through.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Safety Check on Doors, Drawers & Buttons**
>
> - Pad cabinet doors or start with soft-close drawers so nothing slams onto your dog's nose or paws.
> - Use sturdy, non-glass tap lights or talking buttons without small loose battery covers.
> - Check that drawers cannot pull all the way out and drop onto your dog's feet.

## About the Cues

Each component gets its own clean cue first (**Shut It**, **Button**, **Pull**, **Tidy Up**). When you build a chain in Step 5, the previous object's completion becomes the cue for the next step!

## Steps

### Step 1: Pushing a door or drawer closed (`Shut It`).

Transfer your dog's nose-touch from your hand onto a movable surface:

1. Stick a bright sticky note or target coaster onto a lightly ajar cabinet door (or a sturdy plastic box lid). Hold your hand right behind/beside the target if needed at first, then fade your hand away so she touches the sticky note directly.
2. Wait half a second longer for a firmer **nose push** rather than a feather-light tap—click only when the door moves an inch!
3. Gradually leave the door open 4 inches, then 8 inches, clicking only when the door clicks completely shut.
4. Add the cue **Shut It**, then fade the sticky note down to a tiny sliver and remove it completely.

---

### Step 2: Pressing a push-light or talking button (`Button`).

Teaching a clean, controlled press works with either a paw or nose (pick whichever suits your device):

1. Place an unlit battery tap-light or talking button flat on the floor (or taped securely to a heavy board so it doesn't slide).
2. Capture/shape a nose or paw touch onto the center of the button.
3. Hold out slightly for enough downward pressure to make the light turn **on** or the button **speak**—the _click/sound_ of the button itself becomes part of the cue-to-reward loop!
4. Add **Button**, then try moving the button onto a low wall baseboard (with removable command strips) or next to the back door.

---

### Step 3: Pulling a rope or towel (`Pull`).

Teach a gentle, controlled pull without frantic tug-of-war thrashing:

1. Tie a soft, thick braided fleece strap onto a heavy basket handle or a safely anchored low drawer.
2. Hold the free end of the fleece near your dog and encourage her to take it in her mouth. **Click** the moment her teeth close on the cloth.
3. Step slightly back or wait for her to lean her weight backward an inch so the rope goes taut and moves the basket/drawer slightly $\rightarrow$ **Click** and deliver a jackpot from your hand!
4. Add **Pull** as she leans back smoothly, stopping as soon as the drawer opens a few inches.

---

### Step 4: Tidying toys into a basket (`Tidy Up`).

Use **back-chaining** so your dog learns the basket is where the payoff lives:

1. **Start at the Basket (Drop Inside):** Use a wide, low laundry basket. Hold one familiar toy directly _over_ the open basket and ask your dog to take it (or hand-target/retrieve it right above the rim). Say your release cue (**Thank You** / **Drop**) so the toy falls straight into the basket $\rightarrow$ **Click** and jackpot right _over_ the basket!
2. **Pick Up Beside the Basket:** Set the toy 6 inches from the basket on the floor. Cue **Tidy Up**, wait for her to pick it up, lift it over the rim, and drop it inside $\rightarrow$ **Click** and treat.
3. **Add Distance & Multiple Toys:** Gradually move the toy 2 feet, then 4 feet away. Once she banks one toy cleanly, scatter 2–3 toys nearby and wait silently for her to drop one, get paid, and grab the next.

> [!NOTE]
> **Problem: She drops the toy _outside_ the basket**
> Do not click misses! Wait quietly or gently tap the inside rim of the basket once to remind her of the target. If she misses twice in a row, slide back one step (_Chutes and Ladders_) and start closer to the basket.

---

### Step 5: Building a 3-part behavior chain.

Link three already-fluent helper behaviors together (for example: **Pull** a drawer open $\rightarrow$ **Get It** toy out of the drawer $\rightarrow$ **Shut It** to push the drawer closed):

1. **Last Link First:** Open the drawer, place the toy beside it (or in your hand), cue **Shut It**, click and jackpot when the drawer closes.
2. **Add the Middle Link:** Put the toy inside the open drawer. Cue **Get It** (take the toy out and hand it to you or drop it in a basket), immediately cue **Shut It**, then click and jackpot when the drawer closes!
3. **Add the First Link:** Close the drawer with the toy inside. Cue **Pull** $\rightarrow$ as soon as it opens, wait for (or cue) her to grab the toy $\rightarrow$ then **Shut It** $\rightarrow$ **Click** and big celebration at the end!

#### Comeafters

With practice, fade out the middle verbal cues so opening the drawer automatically prompts taking the toy, which automatically prompts shutting the drawer—a true, self-chaining helper sequence.
