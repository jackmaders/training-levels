# Licking & Soothing Activities

Rhythmic licking is one of the fastest ways to help an injured dog settle. The repetitive motion naturally lowers heart rate, releases calming neurotransmitters (endorphins and dopamine), and keeps your dog completely stationary in a comfortable down position.

## 1. Lick Mats

Textured silicone mats slow down eating and turn a small portion of food into 15–25 minutes of quiet focus.

### How to Set Up Safely for Injury Rest

- **Start easy to prevent frustration:** Choose a mat with shallow bumps or grooves first. Deep, intricate honeycomb patterns can frustrate beginners and cause pawing or chewing.
- **Keep it anchored:** Place the mat flat on a non-slip rug right between your dog's front paws, or use a suction-cup/clip-on mat attached to crate bars at **resting chin height** so he does not have to stand or crane his neck.
- **Freeze for extra duration (optional):** Once your dog understands lick mats, chill or freeze the spread mat for 30–60 minutes to extend licking time without adding extra calories.

> [!NOTE]
> **Watch for Chewing**
> Supervise the first few sessions. If your dog tries to chew the silicone mat instead of licking it, pick the mat up calmly before it becomes a hazard and switch to a sturdy rubber feeder (like a Kong or Toppl).

## 2. Stuffed & Frozen Feeders (Kong, Toppl, Tux)

Hollow rubber feeders allow you to serve part or all of your dog's regular meals in a slow, soothing format.

### Progressive Layering (Chutes & Ladders Difficulty)

1. **Level 1 (Easy — No Frustration):** Mix dry kibble with a splash of warm water or low-sodium broth until soft, mash lightly into the toy, and serve at room temperature.
2. **Level 2 (Moderate):** Pack soaked kibble into the bottom two-thirds, seal the opening with a thin smear of plain pumpkin or plain yogurt, and chill in the fridge for an hour.
3. **Level 3 (Long Duration):** Freeze the packed feeder solid (2–4 hours). A fully frozen Toppl or Kong can provide 30–45 minutes of stationary work.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Preventing Pouncing & Rolling**
> Round toys like Kongs can roll out of reach and tempt an injured dog to lunge or stand up. Wedge a Kong inside a clean heavy ceramic bowl, hold a stabilizer base, or use wide-bottomed toys (like a West Paw Toppl or Bowl) that stay put right between your dog's paws.

## 3. Injury-Friendly, Low-Calorie Toppers & Fillings

Keep fillings gentle on the stomach (especially if your dog is on pain medications or antibiotics) and deduct calories from his daily kibble allowance:

- **Soaked Regular Kibble Mash:** Zero extra calories—blend or soak his normal kibble in warm water until it forms a spreadable paste.
- **Plain Canned Pumpkin (100% pure, no pie spice):** High fiber, low calorie, and gentle on digestion.
- **Plain Nonfat Greek Yogurt or Kefir (unsweetened, xylitol-free):** Cooling and creamy; use a thin smear.
- **Unsweetened Applesauce or Mashed Banana:** A tiny spoonful goes a long way for flavor.
- **Low-Sodium, Onion/Garlic-Free Broth Cubes:** Freeze diluted dog-safe bone broth into ice cube trays (or pour a thin layer onto a lick mat and freeze).
- **Canned Wet Dog Food:** Use a portion of his regular wet diet as the "glue" for dry kibble.

> [!CAUTION]
> Always check labels carefully—never use ingredients containing **xylitol (birch sugar)**, onions, garlic, grapes/raisins, nutmeg, or heavy fats, and clear any diet additions with your vet if your dog has medication-related stomach sensitivity.

## 4. Passive Sensory Decompression

Enrichment does not always require food:

- **Breeze & Window "Sniffari":** Crack a window slightly or open a screened door near your dog's resting spot so fresh outdoor scents drift across his nose while he stays safely confined.
- **Porch, Wagon, or Car Rest-Stop:** If your vet allows gentle carrying or lifting into a car/wagon/stroller without putting weight on the injured limb, simply sitting quietly outside for 10 minutes lets your dog watch the world and process outdoor smells with zero walking.
- **Calming Audio:** Soft classical music, acoustic guitar, or calm audiobooks can mask sudden outdoor noises that might otherwise make a confined dog bark or jump up.
