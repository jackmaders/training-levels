# Injury & Crate-Rest Enrichment Reference Guide

When your dog is recovering from an injury or surgery and walks are off-limits, physical restriction does not mean his brain has to shut down. Without an appropriate mental outlet, boredom quickly turns into pacing, whining, scratching at the crate door, or sudden bursts of zoomies that risk re-injury.

The goal of **injury enrichment** is simple: **engage your dog's nose, tongue, and problem-solving brain while keeping his body still, flat, and comfortable.**

## The Five Golden Rules of Recovery Enrichment

1. **Zero Impact & Non-Slip Surfaces:** Keep every activity at bed or floor level inside the crate or recovery pen. Never encourage jumping, twisting, rearing up, or vigorous pawing. Use yoga mats, runners, or grippy bedding so paws never slide.
2. **Calorie Accounting:** Because your dog is burning far fewer calories on rest, **measure out his daily kibble and treat allowance each morning** and use that ration inside his lick mats, puzzles, and training games. Adding extra calories puts unnecessary weight on healing joints and tissues.
3. **Calm Arousal Over Frantic Energy:** Enrichment during recovery should _lower_ heart rate and soothe your dog. If a puzzle causes digging, thrashing, barking, or frantic biting, pick it up immediately and swap to something simpler and calmer (such as a lick mat).
4. **Short Micro-Sessions:** 3–5 minutes of active sniffing or stationary clicker work is plenty. Follow every brain game with a calming lick activity so your dog transitions smoothly into deep rest.
5. **Embrace Sleep:** Tissue repair happens during sleep. You do not need to entertain your dog every waking hour—aim for structured enrichment windows balanced with long, quiet naps.

## Suggested Daily Recovery Rhythm

| Window             | Activity                                                                                                 | Purpose                                                       |
| :----------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Morning**        | Breakfast served in a **towel fold**, **micro-snuffle mat**, or **lick mat**                             | Starts the day with calm foraging instead of a 30-second bowl |
| **Mid-Morning**    | 3–5 min **stationary sniffing** or [Nosework Level 1](../nosework-levels/level-1-foundation-and-odor.md) | Focused olfactory workout without moving the body             |
| **Midday**         | Quiet nap (optional calming music/audiobook)                                                             | Rest and tissue recovery                                      |
| **Afternoon**      | 3-min **stationary clicker / cooperative care** session (chin rest, Zen)                                 | Relationship building & vet-prep skills                       |
| **Late Afternoon** | **Frozen Kong / Toppl** or **porch/car sniffari** (safely confined)                                      | Relieves late-day restlessness safely                         |
| **Evening**        | Dinner via **muffin-tin puzzle** or **stuffed feeder**, followed by bedtime rest                         | Mental fatigue for overnight calm                             |
