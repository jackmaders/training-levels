# Stationary Sniffing & Foraging Games

Sniffing is intensely tiring for dogs because processing scent requires deep mental focus. By bringing foraging setups directly to your dog while he lies down, you give him the mental benefits of a long walk without putting weight or strain on his injury.

> [!TIP]
> **Keep Sessions Short & Calm**
> Run 3–5 minute sniffing games, using dry kibble from his daily meal or tiny soft treats. If your dog starts pawing aggressively, shaking the item, or whining out of frustration, make the setup easier right away.

## Game 1: The Towel "Burrito" & Accordion Fold

A clean bath towel or tea towel is one of the safest, softest foraging tools for a resting dog.

- **Setup (Easy — Accordion Fold):** Lay a towel flat on the bed or floor in front of your dog. Scatter a small handful of kibble across it, then fold the towel loosely in half or accordion-style so pieces sit inside soft pockets.
- **Setup (Moderate — Loose Roll):** Scatter kibble along the length of the towel and roll it loosely like a sleeping bag.
- **How to Play:** Place the folded towel right between your dog's front paws. Hold the far corner gently if needed so the towel stays in place while he uses his nose—not frantic paws—to nudge open the folds and sniff out each piece.

## Game 2: Micro-Snuffle Mat in the Pen

A fabric snuffle mat mimics foraging through grass while your dog stays lying down.

- **Setup:** Use a small, flat snuffle mat with a non-slip backing (or set it on top of a rubber mat).
- **How to Play:** Press dry kibble deep into the fleece strips while your dog watches, then slide the mat within comfortable nose reach. Encourage calm sniffing; pick the mat up as soon as all pieces are found so he doesn't start chewing the fleece.

## Game 3: The Muffin Tin & Ball / Paper Cup Puzzle

A standard 6-cup or 12-cup metal or silicone muffin tin makes an instant stationary puzzle.

- **Setup:** Drop a single kibble or smelly treat into 2 or 3 cups of the tin, then cover **all** cups with lightweight tennis balls, crumpled paper balls, or overturned paper Dixie cups.
- **How to Play:** Hold the tin steady flat on the floor right in front of your resting dog (holding the edge prevents it from sliding or tipping). He must sniff across the covered cups, decide which ones hold food, and gently nudge the right ball/cup aside with his nose.

## Game 4: "Which Hand?" & the 3-Cup Shell Game

Zero extra equipment required—great for quick 2-minute brain breaks inside a crate or on the sofa:

### "Which Hand?"

1. Hide a smelly treat inside one closed fist while keeping your other fist empty (rub both hands gently with the treat first if you want a harder scent challenge).
2. Hold both closed fists side-by-side 2 inches in front of your dog's nose.
3. Ignore pawing or licking at the empty hand. The moment he sniffs or gently bumps the hand holding the treat, mark **"Yes!"** (or click) and open that hand for his reward.

### 3-Cup Shell Game

1. Place 2 or 3 heavy plastic cups (or small yogurt tubs) upside down on a non-slip mat right in front of your dog, with a treat hidden under **one** cup.
2. Let him sniff across the cups. When his nose lingers on or gently nudges the correct cup, mark **"Yes!"** and lift the cup to let him eat the treat.

## Game 5: Supervised Cardboard & Lettuce Shredding

For dogs who naturally enjoy tearing things apart, controlled shredding provides tremendous sensory relief—**provided they spit out pieces rather than swallowing them**.

- **Crumpled Paper Box:** Place a small handful of kibble inside a shallow open box or paper bag, then loosely fill the space around it with crumpled packing paper or junk-mail paper so your dog pokes his nose through the rustling paper to find the food.
- **Egg Carton / Toilet Paper Tubes:** Tuck kibble inside a clean cardboard egg carton (unclosed at first) or fold the ends of an empty paper towel roll loosely inward.
- **Safe Food Shredding (Cabbage / Romaine Head):** For dogs who want to chew and tear, wedge tiny treats between the outer leaves of a clean head of romaine or iceberg lettuce—the "wrapper" is edible and safe!

> [!CAUTION]
> **Swallowing Risk**
> Never give cardboard, paper, or fabric puzzles to a dog who ingests non-food materials, and always supervise closely. If your dog rips off and tries to swallow cardboard or paper, remove it immediately and stick to edible lettuce heads, lick mats, or snuffle mats.
